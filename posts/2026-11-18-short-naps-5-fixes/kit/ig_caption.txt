Short naps can make the whole day feel choppy. Here are 5 steps toward better naps 💤🌤️
Cleveland Clinic suggests getting to know your baby's wake window, the time they're awake between naps, which gets longer as they grow, and putting them down as soon as you see sleepy cues like yawning, eye rubbing or staring off. Calm their senses before the nap: dim the lights and cut the stimulation. From about 4 months, the AAP suggests putting babies down drowsy but not yet asleep, so they learn to fall asleep on their own. And keep nap times as consistent as you can: Cleveland Clinic says a regular sleep-wake schedule helps babies get better at falling asleep and staying asleep. Back to sleep on their back, on a firm, flat surface, in an empty crib, for every sleep until age 1. Which nap runs shortest at yours?
