Better nights start at breakfast ☀️🌳 The NHS says it's a good idea to teach your baby that night is different from day, right from the start: open the curtains, play games, and don't worry too much about everyday noise while they nap. The AAP adds: make daytime playtime, with talking, reading and playing together. Cleveland Clinic suggests bright, sunny spaces by day, keeping naps in the routine (skipping them can leave baby overtired), and lots of holding while they're awake. At night, the NHS suggests low lights and quiet voices, so baby gradually learns night is for sleeping. Back to sleep on their back, on a firm, flat surface, in an empty crib, for every sleep until age 1. How do you spend awake time together?
