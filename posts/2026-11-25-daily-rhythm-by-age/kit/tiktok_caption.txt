Feeds and naps by age, as typical ranges 📋🌼 These come from the AAP, the AASM and Cleveland Clinic, and they're ranges, not targets: the AAP says most healthy, full-term babies can be fed whenever they seem hungry. Newborns: breastfed babies usually nurse about every 2 hours, so 10 to 12 times in 24 hours is the norm, and bottle-fed newborns usually eat every 2 to 3 hours, with 8 feeds in 24 hours generally the minimum, per the AAP. Newborn sleep comes in short stretches, often 1 or 2 hours at a time. By 4 months, Cleveland Clinic says babies usually nap 3 to 5 hours across the day. From 4 to 12 months, some babies take one nap and some take two or three, and Cleveland Clinic says that's OK as long as they're happy, healthy and reaching their total: 12 to 16 hours in 24 hours, naps included, per the AASM. By 6 months, bottle-fed babies may have 4 or 5 feeds in 24 hours, around when solid foods start. From 1 to 2 years, it's likely one nap, with 11 to 14 hours of total sleep.
