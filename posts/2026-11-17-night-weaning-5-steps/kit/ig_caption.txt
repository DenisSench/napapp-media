Night weaning goes best gently, at your baby's pace 🍼✨
The NHS says that for some babies aged 6 months to a year, night feeds may no longer be necessary, and Cleveland Clinic describes night weaning as making sure your baby eats well during the day so they don't need to wake to eat at night. Go gradually: when dropping feeds, the NHS suggests one at a time, so you both have time to adjust. Keep night feeds calm, with the lights low and your voice quiet. And follow your baby's cues: the AAP says you can still feed them at night if needed. Not sure your baby is ready? Cleveland Clinic says night weaning is safe as long as your baby is healthy and at an appropriate weight. Back to sleep on their back, on a firm, flat surface, in an empty crib, for every sleep until age 1. Where are you on the night-feed journey?
