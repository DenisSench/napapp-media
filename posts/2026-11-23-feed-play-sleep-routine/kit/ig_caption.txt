Eat, play, sleep: a gentle daytime rhythm for young babies 🍼🧸
When baby wakes, offer a feed and follow their hunger cues rather than the clock; the AAP calls this responsive feeding and lists cues like rooting, lip licking and hands to mouth. Then play while they're awake: talking, reading and supervised tummy time, which the AAP suggests after a nap or a diaper change. Then sleep, on their back. Why feed first? Cleveland Clinic suggests feeding at the start of the bedtime routine so baby doesn't rely on feeding to fall asleep, and from 4 months the AAP suggests putting baby down drowsy, not already asleep. If you're breastfeeding, the NHS says babies often doze during feeds in the early weeks, so carry on until they've finished. It's a rhythm, not a clock. Back to sleep on their back, on a firm, flat surface, in an empty crib, for every sleep until age 1. Save this and follow for more tips. Napapp logs sleep and feeds in one tap.
