Evening cluster feeds can feel endless, and they're very normal 🌆☕ The NHS says cluster feeding usually happens in the first 3 to 4 months, when your baby wants to feed more often, sometimes constantly, for a while. Cleveland Clinic explains that prolactin, the hormone that tells your body to make milk, is usually lowest in the evening, so babies may feed more often then. Four ways through: if you're breastfeeding, feed on their cues; the NHS says you can't overfeed a breastfed baby. If you're bottle feeding, the NHS says to watch for break cues, like turning their head away or pushing the bottle away, since it's important not to overfeed. Hand off the extras: the NHS suggests a partner brings you dinner if baby wants to feed at the same time, or keeps older kids entertained. And when baby is asleep, on their back in their own crib, try to rest too. Back to sleep on their back, on a firm, flat surface, in an empty crib, for every sleep until age 1.
